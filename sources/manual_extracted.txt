Playable Factory | Product Manager Case Study
You have 2 days. Good luck!
Playable Factory works with both game companies and non-gaming brands and apps. Every task in this case has
a gaming side and a non-gaming side, so we can see how you think across both.
AI is allowed and encouraged in every task. We want to see how you use it, not whether you use it. Task 5 is
dedicated to AI, and it also asks for a short log of how you used AI across the whole case. Using AI will never cost
you points. Submitting unverified or made-up information will.

Task 1 | Identifying New Market Opportunities
Choose two companies that you think could benefit from our services:
    • 1A: a game company
    • 1B: a non-gaming brand or app (any vertical you like)
For each company:
1.1 Explain why you chose this company and how it can benefit from playable ads. For 1B, also explain what the
playable should drive (install, sign-up, first purchase, store visit, etc.) and why that goal fits the brand.
1.2 Provide a step-by-step strategy for how Playable Factory could approach and onboard this company as a
client. Point out what changes between approaching a game company and a non-gaming brand (decision makers,
buying process, objections, success metrics).
1.3 Create a playable ad concept for one of your two companies (gaming or non-gaming, your choice). Outline:
    • Objectives
    • Target audience
    • Key message
1.4 Design a basic wireframe or mockup of the playable ad flow for your 1.3 concept.
Present Task 1 as a short client-facing deck (max 8 slides for both companies combined).

Task 2 | Proposing Product Improvement
Choose one of Playable Factory's products: Insight, Ready, Flex.
2.1 Identify potential areas for improvement or additional features that could enhance its functionality. Show how
your improvement serves both a gaming client and a non-gaming client. If it only fits one, explain the trade-off.
2.2 Prepare a one-page proposal presentation to promote the chosen product.

Task 3 | Evaluating Playable Factory's Playables
3.1 Evaluate the following playable ads created using Playable Studio. For each playable:
    • Provide a brief description of the game mechanics
    • Identify three critical improvements or changes
    • Explain why you would implement them
Playables to evaluate:
    • Squad Busters
    • Clean It!
    • All in Hole
3.2 Pick one of the three mechanics and adapt it for a non-gaming brand of your choice. Describe the brand, the
adapted flow in 3 to 5 steps, and the end-card call to action. Explain what you kept from the original and what you
had to change so it works for a non-gaming audience.

Task 4 | Three Innovative and Engaging Banner Concepts
4.1 Provide three innovative banner concepts designed to captivate and engage users effectively. At least one
must be for a game and at least one for a non-gaming brand. (Text only)
“Banner” here means a static or animated display ad, not a playable.
Task 5 | AI in Your Workflow
Use any AI tools you like (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, etc.). We care about how you guide the tool, how
you check its output, and what you do with it.
5.1 AI-assisted prospecting. Build a list of 10 potential clients:
    •    5 game companies: independent or mid-size studios, not the big publishers (so no Zynga, Scopely or
         similar), each with at least one casual or puzzle mobile game that has recent user acquisition activity in
         the US or Europe.
    • 5 non-gaming companies (each with its app) from a narrow vertical you define yourself (for example
         “grocery delivery apps in MENA” or “European neobanks”). Tell us why you picked that vertical.
For each prospect include: company, app, why it is a good fit, source link, company website and LinkedIn page,
the role you would contact first.
5.2 Raw data to report. This sheet has raw performance data from two playable campaigns: Puzzle Game X (a
puzzle game, conversions = installs) and Grocery App Y (a grocery delivery app, conversions = first orders). Turn
it into a short client-facing report for each client (one page or up to 3 slides each).
The brief is open on purpose. Decide which metrics matter to each client and which to leave out, and explain why.
Also list any metrics we could track inside a playable that are not in this data but would be meaningful to each
client. End each report with 2 or 3 recommendations.
5.3 Designing an AI workflow. Assume you have Sensor Tower API access and an AI assistant that can call it.
In half a page, describe how you would build a repeatable workflow that:
    1. Lists games that match a given target (genre, market, downloads, ad activity)
    2. Finds each publisher's website and LinkedIn page
    3. Identifies the relevant people to contact at each publisher
Include example prompts, which data points you would pull, how you would check quality, and the limits or risks
you see.
5.4 AI log (required, covers the whole case). For each task, tell us briefly:
   • Which AI tools you used and for what
   • Share links or screenshots of your key prompts and conversations
   • One place where the AI output was wrong or weak, and how you caught and fixed it
   • One thing you chose not to use AI for, and why
Keep 5.4 to one page.
