# Task 1.1–1.2: opportunity selection and onboarding

## Recommended starting pair

**Gaming direction: Fugo / Words of Wonders.** Fugo's official site lists Words of Wonders and other casual word titles. A short word puzzle is a plausible playable demonstration because the user can understand and perform one meaningful action quickly. This is a fit hypothesis, not proof of unmet creative needs, current media buying or eligibility for 5.1. Verify ownership/scale and current advertising before reusing Fugo in that task. [S17]

**Non-gaming direction: Picnic / grocery app, initially Netherlands.** Picnic's official app page describes shopping and recipe-to-basket functionality. Use a grocery-list concept to make convenience tangible, with first order as the business objective and installation/account creation as necessary intermediate steps for new users. Do not promise immediate delivery; use market-specific approved service claims. [S20–S22]

Alternative: select another fully qualified studio from Task 5.1 once dated UA evidence is available. Do not delay Task 1's creative planning while searching for a perfect studio. Do not claim that either proposed company is a new-to-PF account without checking PF's known client portfolio or asking sales.

## Selection reasoning to put on slides

For each company give: one supported product fact, one plausible acquisition friction, one playable mechanism addressing it, one business outcome and one uncertainty. Example for Fugo: a visible word mechanic can demonstrate the product before install; test whether authentic word discovery improves qualified-install acquisition. Example for Picnic: a shopping-list task can demonstrate basket-building convenience; test first-order acquisition in confirmed service areas. Avoid market-size filler that does not change the choice.

Prioritize with an internal scorecard, not fabricated precision: mechanic demonstrability, app/business fit, measurable conversion, identifiable buyer, evidence of acquisition activity, and scope feasible for a pilot. Rate high/medium/low and write one reason per rating. The scores are analyst judgment, not measured propensity to buy.

## Stepwise client approach

| Stage | Gaming studio | Grocery app |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Research | Examine actual store listing, visible creative mix, eligible markets and core mechanic | Check service areas, acquisition journey, app experience and approved value propositions |
| 2. Identify sponsor | Head of UA / Growth; Creative Strategist as influential partner | Head of Performance Marketing / Growth; Brand and CRM as partners |
| 3. Open conversation | One specific creative observation and a small mechanic-based test idea | One customer friction and a benefit-led interactive demonstration |
| 4. Discovery | Target CPI, retained-player quality, network mix, creative refresh cadence, assets | First-order CPA, margin after incentive, serviceability, repeat rate, attribution and fulfillment limits |
| 5. Pilot brief | One mechanic, two purposeful variants, one platform/market stratum initially | One basket use case, localized offer only if approved, service-area targeting, two variants |
| 6. Commercial and technical setup | Approve scope, asset rights, delivery format, tracking and decision owner | Add brand/legal review, offer terms, deep-link/landing ownership and campaign operations |
| 7. Build and QA | Tutorial clarity, real mechanic fidelity, asset weight, CTA and event validation | Product clarity, accessible controls, honest benefits, handoff to app/order journey |
| 8. Launch and learn | Evaluate installs with quality guardrails; inspect funnel if performance weak | Evaluate first orders with margin/repeat-order guardrails; inspect post-click friction |
| 9. Expand or stop | Iterate the winning hypothesis before extending markets/networks | Expand only where acquisition economics and fulfillment capacity support it |

## Explicit comparison the manual requests

Gaming buyers are often familiar with playable creative testing, so discussion can focus on production throughput, fidelity and quality-adjusted acquisition. Non-gaming buyers may need a clearer connection between the mini-game and purchase behavior, and more coordination across brand, app product, performance and commercial teams. These are discovery hypotheses, not universal organizational facts.

| Likely objection | Response structure |
|---|---|
| “We already have creative production” | Offer a bounded hypothesis or iteration gap; do not insist they need a replacement |
| “Playables bring low-quality users” | Define retained/valuable customer guardrails before launch |
| “This feels childish/off-brand” | Use a task that demonstrates the product and low-friction visual language; validate with brand owner |
| “We cannot attribute first orders” | Resolve tracking before scaling; keep early results limited to measurable stages |
| “We have limited budget” | Agree a spend/time cap and a minimum evidence threshold; do not invent a pilot quote |

## Pilot decision contract

Specify audience, conversion event, attribution window, baseline creative, randomization where possible, budget cap, test duration rule, owner and pass/fail criteria. Do not nominate a universal sample size. Use baseline conversion and a meaningful minimum effect to plan it. Keep bid strategy, placements and audience as comparable as practical. If a network optimizes exposure rather than randomizing fairly, describe the result as directional.
