# Task 4.1: three non-playable banner concepts

The manual explicitly defines banners as static or animated display ads and requests text only. The following are original concept directions. They can be refined after choosing final clients; no performance claim is implied.

## Concept 1 — game: “The missing word reveals the place”

**Brand/app direction:** Fugo / Words of Wonders, subject to checking approved game imagery. [S17]

**Format:** a short animated display banner, with a readable static final frame.

**Visual sequence:** a few letter tiles connect automatically into one word; a restrained reveal brings a landmark background into focus; the final frame shows app identity and the CTA. The viewer does not drag letters or play inside the unit.

**Copy direction:** “One word. A new discovery.” **CTA:** “Play now.”

**Audience/goal:** puzzle-curious mobile users; installation, with downstream player quality checked separately.

**Why this may work:** it communicates word discovery and visual payoff rapidly, with a single idea rather than a miniature unreadable game board. The creative should represent the actual app experience; do not imply a feature the app lacks.

**Test:** discovery-led copy against a simple challenge-led headline, keeping layout constant. Use CTR diagnostically and CPI/retained-player quality to judge acquisition where available.

## Concept 2 — non-game: “From dinner question to grocery basket”

**Brand/app direction:** Picnic. [S20–S22]

**Format:** animated display banner; static version uses a simple before/after composition.

**Visual sequence:** “What’s for dinner?” appears beside a meal image; ingredients settle into a basket automatically; the final frame presents the app and a clear benefit-led CTA.

**Copy direction:** “Dinner ideas. Groceries together.” **CTA:** “Explore Picnic.”

**Audience/goal:** households in confirmed service areas; first orders rather than banner engagement alone.

**Why this may work:** it links the advertisement to a specific planning problem and shows a real category benefit. Do not use a countdown, discount or delivery promise unless approved and accurate in the target market.

**Test:** meal inspiration versus shopping convenience framing. Evaluate first-order CPA with downstream margin/retention context. A cheap click is insufficient.

## Concept 3 — non-game: “Your preferences, carefully picked”

**Brand/app direction:** Everli. Its official site describes shoppers selecting supermarket groceries and personalized preferences. [S25–S26]

**Format:** static display or subtle two-state animation.

**Visual:** two produce illustrations labeled by intended use (for example, “tonight” versus “later this week”), then a shopper/basket cue. The animation demonstrates choice automatically; it is not an in-banner quiz or selector.

**Copy direction:** “Groceries picked with your plans in mind.” **CTA:** “Shop with Everli.”

**Audience/goal:** shoppers who hesitate about delegating grocery selection; first purchase in eligible service areas.

**Why this may work:** it addresses trust and relevance instead of repeating generic speed messaging. Exact product availability and preference handling must match current service behavior.

**Test:** care/trust framing versus convenience framing with equivalent audience, offer and placement. First-order cost is the business metric; complaint/refund patterns are a useful quality check if data exists.

## Execution checks

Keep one message and one CTA per banner. Do not make viewers perform gameplay to reveal the message. Specify static fallback for animation, legibility at the actual display size, brand visibility and landing-page continuity. Check current placement-specific file/dimension/animation limits before production; Google’s display specifications are a reference, not a universal standard. [S10]

Deliver three short text blocks with format, visual, copy, CTA, audience and rationale. Do not add rendered banner designs to Task 4 unless separately requested. One or two text-only pages is an editorial recommendation, not a manual limit.
